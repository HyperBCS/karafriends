# Changelog

## 0.2.1 (2026-10-30)

- The mouse pointer hides in full screen after 3 seconds without moving. It stays visible while Settings is open.
- Settings has a **Quit** tab.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-29)

The first release published since the fork, so it also carries 0.1.0's changes (listed under 0.1.0 in CHANGELOG.md). The phone remote is rebuilt, the display gets a settings screen and a first-start setup, Joysound goes through its TV app's API and gets pitch bars, and the mics have less delay and keep working through a party.

### Phones

- The phone remote is rebuilt. One search box covers DAM and Joysound, by title or by artist (two chips under the box), in both catalogs or one (a menu at the end of that row, shown while both are on). A pasted YouTube or Niconico link opens the video, and a typed video ID gets an "Open the video with this ID" row. DAM and Joysound songs share one song page. A new phone picks its nickname first.
- Search results from DAM and Joysound come in one list, best match first: an exact title before one that starts with the words, before one that contains them, and shorter titles first. Artist searches are ranked the same way. Kana, kanji, romaji and full-width queries match DAM's readings too ("koi" finds 恋, and "らーめん" finds ラーメン).
- The top bar has the same buttons in the same places on every screen, the current one in the accent: Browse (a ranking icon, while Joysound is on), History, Settings (a gear), and Home at the far right. The page holds still on iPhones, iOS 15 included: only the list in the middle and the open queue scroll. Each screen keeps its scroll position, a page opened from another of its kind shows its own spinner, and the playback controls fit 320px phones.
- The now-playing bar at the foot of the screen opens the queue with a tap or a drag up, and folds it with a tap or a drag down. It shows the song playing under "Now playing", with the singer's whole nickname apart from the artist (sliding when it's too long); with the queue open, its title opens the song's page (for typed-in lyrics). The open sheet is a lighter layer with a grab handle: the queue in a well under "Up next", then a toolbar with a switch between the controls and emotes, a big play button between restart and skip, and the key as one stepper that shows its sign (+2, −1). In supervised mode, a phone that can't control the song says so in one line.
- Tapping a queued song shows who queued it, with a button to its page, Remove, and for an admin, Move to top. Admins reorder the queue by dragging a song's grip (or with the arrow keys on it); a drag held near the top or bottom scrolls the queue, and keeps the right song when the queue changes under it. Queued songs show their source, version and lyrics mode ("Joysound · Guide vocal · Kana"), cut short with "…" when too long. A song page's queue button shows the download's progress as a bar along its foot.
- The profile screen is now Settings (the gear; 設定 in Japanese), in groups (You, This phone, On the TV, Admin): the nickname, Language, Readings, Theme, the lyrics on the TV and the admin sign-in. Each choice is one rounded row with the picked one filled and ticked. Old links to the profile still open it.
- The remote can be switched to Japanese, and picks the phone's language at first. Download failures and refusals are translated too.
- **Readings** (Written, Kana or Romaji) shows song titles and artists as DAM's kana reading, or that reading in romaji, in place of the written name: in search results, artist pages, the queue, history and the screen-reader names. The song page shows the written title and its reading together, and the TV shows a song the way the phone that queued it reads. Joysound gives no readings, so its songs show as written.
- **Theme**: Light, Dark, or Auto (the default), which follows the phone.
- History says when each song was sung, keeps the newest 1,000, and has All and Mine.
- An admin signs in on the phone's Settings with the admin password (see Settings and setup) and stays signed in across restarts; with no password set, Settings says so. An admin's Settings has the Song volume, the Guide melody, and each of the display's mics with its Gain (0 to 200 %), Echo (0 to 100 %) and Noise gate. Each slider shows its value ("Gain 120%") and is named for screen readers.
- Phone notifications are gone: browsers only allow them over HTTPS, and the remote is plain HTTP.
- A phone whose live connection died silently reconnects within 45 seconds, a phone that blocks site storage can still use the remote, and the remote no longer loads Google Fonts.

### The display

- The TV and the phones have a dark look ("Graphite"), with one accent color, larger type and real depth: lists and settings are rounded cards with soft shadows, fields and slider tracks are sunken, and buttons and picked choices are raised. The TV's sliders are a track filled with the accent up to the value, with a solid thumb, instead of Windows' blue dot in a ring; the phone keeps its own slider (iOS's on an iPhone), tinted with the accent.
- A title or artist too long for its place, on the TV or the phone, slides slowly to its end and back, waiting 2 s at each end, instead of ending in "…". It stays still when the device asks for reduced motion (on Windows, Animation effects off).
- The idle screen is full-screen, with a large QR code, "Scan to join" and the address, and what's up next. While a song plays, the QR code alone sits on a small white tile in the top-left corner, clear of the lyrics. Both codes are drawn sharp at any screen size.
- The QR code points at this computer's LAN IP address (the first one, unless another is picked in Settings) instead of `karafriends.local`. The mDNS responder and the `multicast-dns` package are gone: phones that couldn't resolve `.local` names now connect, and nothing listens on 5353/udp.
- `Q` shows the queue as a strip under the video. The strip and the idle list show each song's title, artist and singer (an initial in a circle), in kana or romaji when the phone that queued it reads that way. Semi-transparent notes in a corner say which song starts (with the singer's nickname), is queued or fails, and emotes rise from above the strip.
- The waits on the TV and the phones (the queue, and "Queued, up in …" on a queue button) count down while a song plays and stop while it's paused. They follow where the song really is: from when the TV starts it (not while a DAM song is still loading), after the host moves the video along, while it stalls, and to the video's real length. The TV and every phone agree, even a phone whose clock is off.
- The screens scale with the window, from 720p to 4K, instead of staying at 1080p sizes, and Settings › Display has a UI scale (50 % to 200 %), kept on the display.
- Joysound songs get pitch bars too: the display reads the melody from the song's guide melody as it starts, and draws the same bars as for DAM songs, with each mic's pitch over them. On three real songs the notes match DAM's own melody data over 93–98 % of the sung time. **Pitch bars** in Settings › Display turns them off or back on for both.
- A song the display can't play (a missing or broken file, or a DAM stream that stops for good) is skipped with a "Couldn't play" note instead of freezing the queue. A song queued while nothing plays starts at once instead of up to 5 seconds later.
- The TV no longer blanks or locks while karafriends runs, and starting karafriends again brings the running display to the front instead of opening a second one. A display that keeps crashing waits between reloads and stops after three crashes in a minute, and a reloaded display starts the song that was playing again instead of skipping it.

### Settings and setup

- A first start (no `config.yaml`) opens a setup screen: the DAM and Joysound logins, each checked as it's entered (or Import config), then the mics and speakers, then an admin password, each step skippable. Later launches open on the idle screen, with or without a login. The TV says "Getting YouTube ready…" while the first start downloads ffmpeg and yt-dlp.
- The display has a Settings screen (`S`, or the gear on the idle screen) in five tabs down the left: Accounts, Party, Sound, Display and Advanced. It opens on the tab it was last on, and a dot marks a tab with something to look at: a refused value, a failed sign-in, a restart to do, or what an import changed. Settings take effect while the app runs; only a new port needs a restart. Each field saves when you leave it, with Escape or Close too, and a refused value names its field ("Songs per phone must be…") and keeps Settings open.
- Each login is checked at launch and whenever it changes, with **Test login** to check it by hand, and DAM's or Joysound's songs are on only while their login works: phones quietly leave a catalog that's off out of the search, its song, artist and Browse pages say so, and with neither on, the search takes video links only. If DAM or Joysound can't be reached (the VPN is off), karafriends tries again by itself and the TV says to turn on the VPN to Japan; a refused login says why, in Settings and on the TV. **Use DAM** and **Use Joysound** (`damEnabled` and `joysoundEnabled` in `config.yaml`, on by default) turn a catalog off whatever its login.
- The proxy settings are gone: outside Japan, run a VPN to Japan on the computer. An old `config.yaml` or an imported file loses `proxyEnable`, `proxyHost`, `proxyPort`, `proxyUser` and `proxyPass`, with a note.
- Guests become admins with an admin password set in Settings › Party (`adminPasswordSalt` and `adminPasswordHash` in `config.yaml`). `adminDeviceIds` and `adminNicks` are gone; device IDs still decide whose song is whose. Setting or changing the password signs every admin phone out. **Everyone is an admin** (`everyoneIsAdmin`) gives every phone the admin controls at once, with no password, for parties; the per-phone song limit still applies.
- Settings › Party › Phones has the Address the QR code points at and the Port. The Address lists only addresses phones can open (no IPv6, VPN or virtual adapters) and keeps the pick across restarts; **Custom URL…** is for phones that reach the computer another way (Tailscale, a port forward, a reverse proxy), and the QR code and the address under it point there until a LAN address is picked again. The `offkai.karafriends.party` choice is gone.
- Settings › Advanced has Downloads (how many files and how much space they take, and Clear downloads, which keeps only the files in use), Tools (the ffmpeg and yt-dlp versions, and Update tools, which downloads them again), and Export config and Import config for the whole config, logins included. The display says when YouTube downloads fail because of yt-dlp rather than the video. `KARAFRIENDS_YTDLP_PATH` and `KARAFRIENDS_FFMPEG_PATH` point the app at your own copies.
- Importing an old `config.yaml` reads its `YOUR_PASSWORD_HERE` as empty, lists anything it couldn't use, tests both logins, and says when phones must sign in again. A `config.yaml` that isn't settings is kept as `config.yaml.broken`, Settings says so and where, and the logs never print a line of it. A key given twice is no longer fatal, `off`/`no` and all-digit passwords are read as written, and `config.yaml` is flushed to disk before it replaces the old one.

### Songs and catalogs (DAM, Joysound, YouTube, Niconico)

- Joysound goes through the JOYSOUND TV app's API instead of sound-cafe.jp's web pages. karafriends links itself to the Joysound account as its TV app the first time it's used (with the saved email and password), keeps the link encrypted in its own folder, and links again only if Joysound ends it; a new email drops the old account's link. Joysound links one TV app at a time: when another holds the link, karafriends leaves it be and says so, and Test login offers **Switch the TV link to karafriends**. Test login says how the link stands without linking anything. Joysound's songs need a paid member's account: with a free one, the line under the login says so and phones don't show Joysound. A preview is never kept as the whole song.
- **Browse** (the ranking icon in the phone's top bar, while Joysound is on) lists Joysound's newest songs, its 16 genres' most popular songs, and its featured lists with their pictures, kept for an hour. DAM has no such lists karafriends can use. A Joysound artist's page lists the songs most popular first and says how many songs the artist has.
- A DAM or Joysound song's page shows a picture, fetched by the computer: DAM's karaoke video title card, or Joysound's album art from Amazon or Apple. DAM songs show their length, and a DAM song with no tie-up no longer says "DAM · undefined".
- A Joysound song's lyrics show on the TV with kana over the kanji, in romaji, or both (romaji under the kanji line), picked on its page when queueing, with the phone's default under On the TV in its Settings; the romaji queue button is gone. The romaji sits centred under what it reads, on a 16:9 screen too. A Joysound song's background video is one of its five, picked at random, instead of always the first.
- Joysound's guide melody, which was never heard before, can be turned up with **Guide melody** in Settings › Sound or an admin's phone (muted by default, kept across songs and restarts).
- A Joysound song with a guide-vocal version (another recording, with a sung vocal on the guide channel) can be queued as that, from a second button on its page. Its guide vocal plays at the guide melody's level, muted until raised, and it has no pitch bars.
- Joysound songs sound as Joysound made them: Chromium decoded Joysound's 3-channel sound wrong wherever the guide melody plays, garbling the guide, the vocals and the backing alike, as if underwater. Downloads now decode it with ffmpeg and keep it as Opus at 256 kb/s, so a song takes about 20 MB instead of 15. Joysound songs downloaded before are downloaded again the next time they're queued.
- A DAM song that played only a short clip every time now plays whole: a DAM download much shorter than the song (DAM's sample) is refused with "DAM sent a sample, not the song" and the song streams, and such a file left from before is deleted when it comes to play.
- A DAM stream rides out a passing hiccup or a brief connection drop instead of skipping the song, moves on when it ends without the usual signal, and after three stalls in a song moves to the low bitrate for the rest of it.
- A failed Joysound, YouTube or Niconico download tells the phone that queued it why, on its queue button ("Couldn't download: Video unavailable"), the TV shows it too, and the song can be queued again at once. Live, upcoming and paid videos say so instead of blaming the tools. Downloads that hang are stopped after 15 minutes.
- DAM and Joysound give up within seconds on a wrong password or a connection from outside Japan instead of retrying for 17 minutes, and log in again by themselves when a session ends. Joysound searches say what went wrong, with Retry, instead of "No results".

### Sound and microphones

- The mics no longer cut out after a while with "input fell behind" (a mic and an output at different rates, such as a USB mic and the TV over HDMI, lost samples on every callback). All mics now play through one **Mic output**, picked in Settings › Sound (the system default, or any output device, ASIO included in every Windows build), open at its rate when they can, never replay stale sound, and follow the two clocks' drift one sample at a time. The songs play through their own **Song output**.
- The mics have less delay: each holds only what plays without gaps (about 15 ms for a USB mic into an ASIO output, instead of 50 to 150 ms), and keeps a little more spare each time it runs dry. A mic resampled for an output at another rate is about 4 ms late instead of 24.
- Settings and the phones show each mic by its name ("Yeti Stereo Microphone (USB), channel 1") instead of an ID; mics saved before still open. Each mic has Gain and Echo sliders, a **Noise gate**, a level meter, and its own Remove button instead of "Clear mics"; its levels are kept with it and an admin's phone changes them live. The noise gate is off until raised: under it, the mic stays quiet on the speakers and draws no pitch line, so small noises don't come through, and the meter marks it.
- A mic that stops is retried every 2 s until it's back, with the reason in its row; a mic or mic output that goes silent without an error is noticed and reopened, and a capture glitch no longer stops a mic. On macOS and Windows, a mic unplugged and plugged back in comes back by itself. The mics move back to the chosen Mic output within about 10 seconds of its return. A mic or output that won't open no longer breaks the display at the next launch: the default output plays instead, and Settings says why. A saved mic that's busy at launch opens once it can, and mics that aren't plugged in stay saved. A mic that is removed or changed lets its device go at once.
- Settings lists the devices again each time it opens, a mic plugged in while it's open shows under Add a microphone at once, a device that won't give its name or an input that won't answer is left out instead of emptying the list, and a saved output that isn't connected says so (System default clears it).
- Windows with ASIO: an ASIO mic output has a Buffer size and a Sample rate (the driver's own until picked); the chosen buffer holds when the mics are on the same interface, buffers of 4096 and 8192 samples don't crackle, and 24-bit devices open. An ASIO driver that fails no longer hides the other mics, and a line under Mic output says which won't load and why. Opening Settings › Sound no longer stops the open mics: listing the ASIO devices loaded every driver, which stopped the open WASAPI mics, so the ASIO devices are listed again only while no stream is open.
- Windows mics can be Shared or Exclusive (less delay, where Windows allows it), with a Mic buffer for exclusive mode; a mic Windows won't give stays shared and says why, and two mics on the channels of one interface share its stream. "In use" under the output and each mic says the rate and buffer it runs at, the packets Windows lost, and a refused real-time priority.
- **Song volume** (0 to 100 %) in Settings › Sound and on an admin's phone applies to every song; it's back at 100 % at each launch. Joysound songs play at their own volume, not the last song's.
- An **audio/video offset** (−300 to +300 ms) in Settings › Sound, with the measured output latency: plus delays the sound, minus delays the Joysound lyrics and the pitch bars (not YouTube captions or typed-in lyrics; DAM's lyrics are in its video and can't move). It's kept on the display.
- If the song output goes away mid-song (a TV on standby, an HDMI blip), the song keeps going silently and plays there again when it's back, and an output connected after launch is used at once.
- Changing the key moves every note by the interval chosen, bass included (±1 used to leave everything below about 200 Hz where it was), with no high hiss and no slowdown over a long party, and a centred voice or guide melody stays clear and centred. A song in its own key skips the pitch shifter, so it's no longer a little late, plays as loud as with the key changed, and has no dropout when the key moves off 0.

### Platforms

- Linux: the mics and the mic output use PipeWire where it runs (else PulseAudio, else ALSA) with real-time threads, at a **Mic buffer** picked in Settings, 128 samples (2.7 ms) by default. Settings says which sound system the mics use, and `KARAFRIENDS_AUDIO_HOST` picks one by hand. The mic list has only the mics (it listed every ALSA plugin device, over a hundred entries), by their names, ending "(Linux)".
- Linux: the app starts on Ubuntu 22.04 and Mint 21 (PipeWire 0.3.48), where the mics use PulseAudio with a 512-sample buffer and Settings says they aren't real-time. Outputs are no longer listed as mics when PipeWire is slow to answer. When PipeWire stops answering for a while, the mics keep to it: Settings says it isn't answering, and the display asks again every 10 seconds, instead of a black screen at launch, a slow Settings, and a fall back to ALSA's device names. The display paints before it opens any sound device, and a sound-system call that takes a second or more is logged.
- Linux: a USB mic unplugged mid-song shows as gone ("The mic is gone (unplugged?)") and comes back by itself when it's plugged in again; it used to stay "ok", recording silence. The README says what Linux needs to run and to build, and how to fix the sandbox error that stops the app at launch on Ubuntu 23.10 and newer.
- macOS: the mics reach the speakers about 17 ms sooner: the mic output runs at the mics' 128-sample buffer instead of the device's own 512. On a MacBook Pro's own mic and speakers, mic to speaker went from 78 ms to 60. `cargo run --release --manifest-path native/Cargo.toml -p karafriends-lib --example mic_latency` measures it through the speakers.
- Tools: Linux gets yt-dlp's standalone build (no Python needed), and Apple Silicon a native ffmpeg (no Rosetta), which checks HTTPS certificates against macOS's own (`/etc/ssl/cert.pem`, unless `SSL_CERT_FILE` is set). Niconico songs download where ffmpeg isn't installed system-wide. A downloaded tool must start before it replaces the old one, a failed update doesn't fail the downloads waiting on it, and on Windows, Update tools works while a download runs.
- Downloads and the saved queue live in the app's data folder instead of the temp folder (cleared at boot on many Linux systems); a queue saved by an older version moves over once.
- The release zips unzip into a `karafriends-<platform>` folder.
- macOS: the zip is for Apple Silicon Macs only; Intel Macs are no longer built for.
- macOS: the zip is signed and notarized, and the signed app's mics work: under the hardened runtime a process needs the microphone entitlement to hear anything, and the display's renderer, where the mics open, didn't have it. macOS says why karafriends wants the microphone when it asks.
- Linux: the display window has karafriends' icon.
- ffmpeg is one pinned build per platform (FFmpeg 9.0.2 on Windows and macOS, Jellyfin's portable 8.1.3 on Linux), and yt-dlp is still the latest release. Every tool download must match its SHA-256 before it's unpacked or run; one that doesn't is deleted, and the tool already there stays. Linux points ffmpeg at the system's CA certificates. Updating from an earlier version downloads ffmpeg once more. The Windows build checks the ASIO SDK against a pinned checksum.
- The README says what Windows SmartScreen asks on a first start, and that closing the console window beside the app closes karafriends.

### Development

- `yarn dev` keeps its settings, logins, queue and downloads in the app's own folder (`%APPDATA%\karafriends` on Windows) instead of one named `Electron`, and no longer opens every saved mic twice. On Linux, `yarn dev` starts Electron with `--no-sandbox`, so it runs where Chromium's sandbox helper isn't set up (Ubuntu 23.10 and later). Under `yarn dev`, the Port field says phones use the dev server's port.
- The end-to-end tests run side by side, as many at once as half the computer's threads (26 seconds instead of 5½ minutes on a PC with 32 threads), and each closes the app in a tenth of a second. `yarn test:native` runs plain `cargo test`; CI still runs the native tests under cargo-careful (`yarn test:native:careful`). The downloader has unit tests with fake ffmpeg and yt-dlp.
- CI builds faster, installs only Chromium for the end-to-end tests, starts the packaged Linux zip on Ubuntu 22.04 for every change, and runs the Windows-only Rust tests. Upstream's unused Cloudflare tunnel and DAM decryptor files are gone.
- Dependencies: Electron 45 (Chromium 156, Node 24.22), TypeScript 7 (the native compiler; `yarn typecheck` takes under a second), React 19.3, youtubei.js 18.3 and every other npm package at its latest; every Rust crate at its latest (rubato 5 resamples the mics sample-for-sample as before) and the native crates on Rust edition 2024.

### Fixes

- The display window stays on its page: a link, or a file dropped on it, no longer takes it elsewhere, and it opens no other windows. A packaged app ignores `ELECTRON_IS_DEV`.
- The log no longer shows DAM's login code and token. On macOS and Linux, `config.yaml` (which holds the logins) and the Joysound link are readable only by their user.

- Songs are no longer dropped or deleted when two phones pick the same one: the second hears "Someone is already adding this song", one failed Joysound background no longer deletes another, and a DAM song no longer downloads twice into one file. A song is no longer queued half-written after a crash.
- A Joysound download that fails halfway also stops its other half (the YouTube background or the Joysound video) and cleans up after both. At most two YouTube or Niconico downloads and two DAM downloads run at once, and a stopped one stops for good. Half-finished downloads are cleaned up at launch, quitting stops running downloads, and a song still downloading when the app restarted downloads again.
- A Skip, Pause, key change or lyric line sent just as a song ends no longer lands on the next song. When the queue runs out, the last song's lyrics and pitch bars stop instead of running behind the idle screen.
- The pitch bars hide instead of taking the display down when the graphics driver resets or WebGL2 is missing, and come back with the GPU; a DAM song without scoring data shows no empty band; the singer's pitch is found in the mic's newest 25 ms, so low notes hold steady; and announcements and Settings no longer wipe the mic trace.
- Joysound lyrics keep only the lines on screen in GPU memory (about 1 GB less per song on a 4K TV, and no stall at song start), a line of only spaces no longer leaves a song without lyrics, title cards sit below the pitch bars, and the romaji dictionary (100 MB) loads only once a romaji song is queued.
- On the phone, Back keeps working after picking a Joysound background video, and Back from the lyrics screen no longer returns to it while the song plays. An artist with one song says "1 song". F5's dialog on the display no longer freezes the phones.

## 0.1.0 (not released on its own; its changes ship in 0.2.0)

The hard fork: karafriends stops following emmaworley/karafriends and gets simpler underneath. Parties work as before.

- karafriends is now its own project at [HyperBCS/karafriends](https://github.com/HyperBCS/karafriends), building its own Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Linux zips (the first published on its Releases page are 0.2.0's).
- The repository is about 60 MB instead of 2.4 GB, and the app bundle is smaller.
- The app no longer sends crash reports or telemetry anywhere (Sentry and OpenTelemetry are gone).
- Phones and the display get the queue, now playing, playback and download progress the moment they change, instead of checking every few seconds.
- The display keeps working when the phones' port (8080) is taken by another program, and shows a banner saying phones can't connect.
- Admin rights are checked by the app, not by the phone. Admins are listed by device ID; a nickname no longer makes someone an admin.
- Phones no longer see other phones' device IDs; each phone just knows which songs are its own.
- The saved queue (`queue.json`) has a new format. Queues saved by older versions still load.
- A slow or silent DAM or Joysound server no longer holds up or drops the next song, and DAM results page correctly past the first page.
- Song IDs from phones are checked, so a bad one can't touch files outside the app's temp folder.
- Niconico lookups use yt-dlp (at most two lookups run at once), and a YouTube song's typed-in lyrics stay with it while it's queued.
- The phone remote works on older iPhones (Safari 15 and later), and the display and remote load faster with lighter styling.
